## Notes, failures and fixes

- **All ten parts meshed and solved with the existing Quick settings on the first attempt** (task 012); no refinement level, feature angle or layer setting had to change for this corpus. Cells then ranged from 77,000 (drone arm) to 497,000 (wing with winglet), all well under the 1.5 million cap; reading and tessellating a STEP file took 0.4-0.6 s; meshing 3-20 s; the solver 8-56 s for the runs that settled. Since task 013 the three parts with thin plates or fins (rocket, 2 mm plate, beam flange) get a refinement band and more cells; see the before/after table and the last notes.
- **Boundary layers formed to 77-100 % of their planned thickness** on every part (the page warns below 60 %); the lowest coverage is on the long thin beam (77 %) and the drone arm (79 %), where the layers thin out around the small fillets and the clamp plate.
- **Wing with winglet: one failed checkMesh test** - a single face with skewness 4.1 (OpenFOAM's limit is 4) in the sharp corner where the winglet meets the wing tip. The run completed normally; the page shows 'The mesh failed 1 quality check; results may be less accurate'. This is the one place in the corpus where a cleaner CAD model (a small fillet at the junction) would improve the mesh. It is also the biggest mesh (497k cells): the reference length is the chord (0.15 m) but the span is 0.42 m, so the refinement boxes are long in y.
- **L-section block with the re-entrant corner: the solver ran to its 1500-iteration cap** (254 s) without settling. The block is a bluff body with a massively separated, unsteady wake; a steady RANS solver cannot settle on it and the page says so ('the numbers did not fully settle', 'the lift kept swinging'). Its CD of 1.11 (on its frontal area, 0.008 m², the same as its box) is in the range of a cube (1.05) and a square plate (1.17). This is physics, not a meshing failure; the same happens with the built-in cylinder.
- **Rocket: the first version of the generator exposed the 'assembly export' case by accident.** Its fins only touched the tube along a line (no overlap), so the STEP file held 5 separate solids. WindLab fused nothing (there is nothing to fuse), explained that the file holds 5 bodies that do not touch, used the largest (the tube alone, 42 cm long) and solved it; the STL twin of that file was correctly refused ('4 edges are shared by more than two triangles'). The generator now draws the fin roots 5 mm into the tube, as a CAD user would, and the STEP reads as one solid (fins included: 737 triangles, about 86,600 cells, CD 0.65 on its frontal area of 0.00159 m²; the first corpus run quoted 0.0206 on the 0.0504 m² plan-view box, the default reference area until task 012). Lesson for the handout: wings, fins and brackets must be merged into one body (SolidWorks: Insert > Features > Combine) before export.
- **Two-body assembly**: uploaded with 'Use all bodies' - both the cylinder and the box are in the tunnel, the forces are summed and the reference area is the plan-view box around both. Meshed and solved without trouble (93k cells). With the default 'largest body' setting only the cylinder would have been used, with the explanation shown on the page.
- **2 mm flat plate edge-on**: 12 triangles, meshed cleanly with 100 % layer coverage (193k cells) in the task-012 run; its faces lie on the background grid, so even at 1.3 surface cells across (the Quick surface cell is 2L/256 = 1.56 mm here, not the 0.8 mm an earlier version of this note said) it was meshed symmetrically (CL -3e-6). Since task 013 it counts as thin (under 1.5 cells) and gets the refinement band like the rocket's fins; see the before/after table.
- **Large (2 m) and small (30 mm) parts** behave like the mid-size ones because the whole mesh scales with the reference length; the 2 m beam at 20 m/s reaches Re 2.7 million and y+ up to 520 (wall functions cope, but the surface cell is 16 mm there), the 30 mm knob Re 41,000 and y+ 0.2-9.
- **STEP against STL**: every part's STL twin (written by OpenCASCADE at 0.05 mm chord error, like a fine SolidWorks STL export) passed the same geometry checks as the STEP file (9 of 10 at first; 10 of 10 after the rocket fix). The STEP route has the advantage of the declared units and of merging touching bodies; the STL route needs the student to pick the units.
- **Messages seen on the way**: 'N triangles have zero area; they will be ignored' (OpenCASCADE's tessellation of the poles of spheres and cone tips produces a few degenerate triangles; harmless), the 5-body explanation for the first rocket, and the unsteady-flow notes above. No run produced the 'mesh could not be fitted' or 'became unstable' messages, so those paths are covered by the existing unit tests only.
- **Reference areas (task 012, step 2).** Every part's coefficients now sit on the engineer's convention: the **frontal area** for a body or the **planform area** for a wing-like part, both measured from the surface (`windlab.geometry.reference` rasterises the silhouette; the 'ref. area' column shows which). The forces did not change, only the area they are divided by, so CD values moved: rocket 0.0206 on the 0.0504 m² plan-view box -> **0.654 on the 0.00159 m² frontal silhouette** (tube plus four fins; the 0.0144 m² frontal box would give 0.072); car 0.139 on 0.072 m² -> **0.415 on 0.0238 m²** (a bluff model car with a rounded cabin and wheels; real cars 0.25-0.35); bracket 0.48 -> 1.00, beam 0.053 -> 1.22, knob 0.225 -> 0.358, drone arm 0.033 -> 0.74, reentrant block 1.11 -> 1.11 (its two areas are nearly equal). The wing with winglet is guessed as a wing (planform 0.0613 m² against the 0.0632 m² box): CD 0.0228, CL 0.106. The 2 mm plate edge-on counts as a wing (planform 0.02 m²).
- **Rocket at 0 degrees: a lift and side force that are not physics (task 012, step 3).** The symmetric 4-fin rocket gives CL -0.58 and a side force Cs +0.6 on its frontal area (|CL| about 90 % of CD), perfectly converged (cl_pm 4e-5). Probes (scripts in `build/scratch/`, results in `robustness/rocket_symmetry.json`): turning the rocket 90, 180 and 270 degrees about x (which maps the four fins onto themselves) and remeshing gave the same force vector (CL -0.013 to -0.016, Cs +0.018 on the plan-view box) each time, pointing between two fins, so the effect does not turn with the body; the Standard mesh (240k cells) kept it (CL -0.016, Cs -0.016). The finless body (cone nose, flat base) gave CL = Cs = 0.0000 in both orientations, as did a finless spindle; the finned body with a boat-tail gave CL = Cs = +0.019 again. So the blunt base is not the trigger: **the 2 mm fins, thinner than a Quick surface cell (3.3 mm for this 42 cm part: 0.6 cells across), are not meshed symmetrically** by snappyHexMesh (castellation, snapping and layers differ from fin to fin), and a steady RANS solution locks onto the resulting lopsided fin camber. The page and the report said so from task 012 on (an upload that WindLab finds mirror-symmetric top to bottom gets a note that its lift at 0 degrees is mesh noise with the size of the effect); task 013 fixed the cause, below.
- **Thin features refined automatically (task 013).** The upload check now measures the local thickness of every closed part (`windlab.geometry._stl_worker.thickness`: from the centre of up to 4,000 triangles, chosen by area, a ray is cast inwards along the normal; the distance to the first face it meets is the thickness there, and a hit on a face within 12 degrees of parallel is 'plate-like' - a fin, a plate or a wall, as opposed to the sharp wedge of a trailing edge). Rays stop at a tenth of the part's size; the scan takes 0.01-0.4 s on the corpus parts and 4 s on a 457,000-triangle surface, under a 10 s cap. For a given mesh, a plate-like feature thinner than **1.5 surface cells** that covers at least 1 % of the surface is thin (`windlab.geometry.thin`): its triangles (the sampled thin ones, grown to their unsampled neighbours on big meshes) are written as `constant/triSurface/thin.stl` and snappyHexMesh refines every cell within two fine cells of them (a `distance` refinement region) by one level, or by two when one still leaves the cells bigger than the feature. The rocket's fins (2 mm, 28 % of the surface, 0.6 cells across) get one level: 1.6 mm cells. **Options tried on the rocket** (Quick, 4 ranks; `robustness/thin_features.json`): the mesh before task 013 gave CL -0.44 to -0.58 against CD 0.65 (run-to-run), 86,600 cells, 15 s. snappyHexMesh's own gap refinement (`gapLevel (3 7 10)`, `gapMode inside`) only marks cells that see both faces of a fin, which stops once the cells are about the fin's size: 92,800 cells, CL -0.58, no better. The refinement band at level 9 (1.6 mm cells, 1.2 across the fin): **CL +0.003 (0.6 % of CD), CD 0.557, 138,000 cells, 24 s (1.6x)** - the acceptance was |CL| below 0.05 or 5 % of CD within 2x the time. Level 10 (0.8 mm cells): CL 0.00003, CD 0.540, 342,000 cells, 54 s (3.6x); the same with a band only one fine cell wide: 340,000 cells, 48 s - the width hardly matters, the level does. So one level is the default and two the cap. The drag also fell from 0.65 to 0.55-0.54 (the pressure part from 0.38 to 0.29): the coarse fins carried spurious pressure drag as well as the spurious lift. A whole-part surface level of 10 was not tried: the band already costs 3.6x at that level on a part whose fins are a quarter of its surface, and a car or drone with small fins would pay the same price everywhere. **Still rough**: a feature thinner than the finest band cell (for the rocket, under 1.6 mm with one level or 0.8 mm with two) is reported before the run ('close to the smallest cells WindLab uses ... try More accurate, slower') and after it. The symmetric-part note on the result stays, but only when |CL| is at least 0.01 and 5 % of CD. Built-in wings and bodies never get the band: the NACA 0012 accuracy guard was re-run after the change (validation/performance.md).
- **The corpus after task 013** (the before/after table): three of the ten parts have thin plate-like features on the Quick mesh and got the band, seven are untouched (their cells, times and coefficients differ from the task-012 run only by meshing noise: cells within 1 %, CD within 1 %, the bracket's CL by 0.03 on a bluff body whose lift swings). **Rocket**: 86,600 -> 138,000 cells, 16 -> 24 s, **CL -0.584 -> +0.004 (0.7 % of CD)**, CD 0.654 -> 0.558; the symmetric-part note no longer appears (it needs |CL| of at least 0.01 and 5 % of CD). **2 mm plate edge-on** (1.3 cells across, 97 % of its surface thin): the band triples its cells (193,000 -> 640,000) and time (26 -> 96 s) for no visible gain - its CL was already 5e-6 and CD moved 2 % (0.0220 -> 0.0224) - because a grid-aligned plate happens to be meshed symmetrically even at 1.3 cells; WindLab cannot tell such a plate from a fin, and the Standard-mesh rocket showed that 0.95 cells across is not safe, so the 1.5-cell trigger stays and a large thin plate pays for it. **2 m beam's 20 mm flange** (1.3 cells of the 15.6 mm surface cell, 12 % of the surface): 98,000 -> 183,000 cells, 16 -> 38 s, CD 1.22 -> 1.13 and CL 0.004 -> -0.012 - the flange's edges were two cells apart before, so the refined value is the one to trust, within the usual Quick-mesh caveats. A cell budget for very large thin areas (task 014, next note) now spares the plate.
- **The band's cell budget (task 014).** Before meshing, WindLab estimates the cells the band would add (`windlab.geometry.thin.band_cells`): the band itself (two fine cells deep on the fluid side of every thin face, less the surface cells it replaces), the extra boundary-layer cells (5 per surface face, and a face at the finer level is 4x smaller, so 4x as many: the largest part of the cost) and the ring of 3 coarser cells the 2:1 rule pushes outwards, all proportional to the measured thin area. Checked against the task-013 runs: the plate's band added 447,000 cells (estimate 426,000), the rocket's 52,000 (44,000), the beam's 85,000 (21,000: the band also caught the flange's upper face, 20 mm away, whose samples were not plate-like because its triangle centroids look down into the web; so the estimate is a coarse guard, not a prediction, and the preset's hard cap of 1.5 million cells stands behind it). Above **300,000** estimated cells the band gets one level less; when one level is still over, the mesh is left plain, and the 'Thin parts' note, the run's warnings and the report's Mesh section say so with the numbers. Re-run of the two parts (table above, `results_task013.json` is the 'before'): **2 mm plate** 639,893 -> 193,367 cells and 96 -> 26 s (the task-012 mesh again), CD 0.0224 -> 0.0220, CL -3.5e-6; **rocket** unchanged within meshing noise (138,221 -> 138,147 cells, 24 s, CD 0.558 -> 0.559, **CL +0.0034 = 0.6 % of CD**, so the fix holds). The beam flange (estimate 21,000 cells) keeps its band. Decided without the project owner: the budget is a constant (`thin.CELL_BUDGET`), not a preset setting, and the estimate uses the sampled thin area as it is (no doubling for unsampled partner faces, which would overshoot the plate and the rocket, whose faces are all sampled, by 2x).
